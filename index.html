Development Economics and Digital Infrastructure

I am an M.A. student in Economics at Sogang University, advised by Professor Hyunjoo Yang. My research interests lie in development economics and applied microeconomics, with a focus on digital infrastructure, local economic development, spatial inequality, and economic measurement using geospatial and satellite data.

My current research, with Hyunjoo Yang, examines how mobile broadband upgrading during South Africa's 2021–2024 spectrum transition differs across municipalities by initial service quality, baseline poverty, and regulatory priority status. Using quarterly Ookla measurements, we track low-speed service, average performance, and high-speed expansion to distinguish local improvements from changes in gaps across places.

I work as a Research Assistant with the Sogang Spatial Economics & AI Lab and MIRACLE, contributing to research using satellite imagery, geospatial data, and historical records.

Background

I am currently pursuing an M.A. in Economics at Sogang University and expect to graduate in 2027.

Before graduate study in economics, I received a B.A. in South African Languages and Cultures from Hankuk University of Foreign Studies, with a minor in Political Science and Diplomacy. My undergraduate training focused on Southern African regional studies, politics, and international relations.

I also studied at the University of Botswana as an exchange student, where I took coursework in political science. These experiences contributed to my interest in empirical research on development and economic change in African settings.
