Working Paper · October 2026

Mobile Broadband Upgrading and Spatial Inequality in South Africa

With Hyunjoo Yang

How do mobile broadband improvements differ across places with different initial quality and poverty levels? We follow South Africa's 2021–2024 spectrum transition using quarterly Ookla measurements, comparing municipalities by initial service quality, baseline poverty, and ICASA-designated priority status.

Initially slow municipalities reduced their low-speed disadvantage and relative speed gap, while their absolute Mbps gap grew. Priority areas also reduced their low-speed disadvantage, alongside widening gaps in average speed and high-speed tile shares. A separate, exploratory comparison associates higher baseline poverty with less high-speed expansion; its precision varies with inference and measurement coverage.

The findings distinguish progress within underserved places from changes in their position relative to other places. Evaluating upgrading calls for tracking weak-service reduction, average performance, and high-speed expansion together. These comparisons describe the distribution of observed upgrading during the transition.

Data & Period
Quarterly Ookla measurements for South African municipalities, 2021–2024; baseline poverty and ICASA Batch 3 priority classifications.
Empirical Approach
Municipal panel comparisons of low-speed service, average performance, and high-speed expansion; separate initial-quality, priority-area, and poverty comparisons with coverage and inference checks.

Working Paper · Draft

Where Do International Connectivity Gains Arrive?

Spatial Broadband Upgrading and Employment after Equiano in South Africa

This paper studies how post-2023 broadband quality improvements were distributed across South African municipalities following the activation of the Equiano submarine cable, and whether a similar spatial pattern emerged in formal employment.

I combine municipality-level broadband measures from Ookla and Measurement Lab with sectoral PAYE employment data, geospatial distance measures, and satellite-derived controls. The empirical analysis uses municipality fixed effects, event-study diagnostics, alternative spatial gradients, and synthetic-control exercises.

I find that municipalities farther from the Melkbosstrand–Cape Town axis experienced smaller relative improvements in broadband quality after 2023. The pattern appears across both Ookla and M-Lab measures, but the landing point is geographically inseparable from Cape Town-centered economic geography. I therefore interpret the evidence as a spatially unequal broadband adjustment rather than a clean causal effect of Equiano. I do not find a comparably robust spatial pattern in short-run formal employment.

Research Question
How are gains from major digital infrastructure expansions distributed across places, and to what extent are changes in connectivity reflected in local economic outcomes?
Data
Municipality-level broadband performance data, administrative employment data, GIS-based spatial measures, and satellite-derived indicators.
Empirical Approach
Fixed-effects panel models, event studies, spatial exposure and falsification designs, and synthetic-control diagnostics.
