Mobile Broadband Upgrading and Spatial Inequality in South Africa

With Hyunjoo Yang

How do mobile broadband improvements differ across places with different initial quality and poverty levels? We follow South Africa's 2021–2024 spectrum transition using quarterly Ookla measurements, comparing municipalities by initial service quality, baseline poverty, and ICASA-designated priority status.

Initially slow municipalities reduced their low-speed disadvantage and relative speed gap, while their absolute Mbps gap grew. Priority areas also reduced their low-speed disadvantage, alongside widening gaps in average speed and high-speed tile shares. A separate, exploratory comparison associates higher baseline poverty with less high-speed expansion; its precision varies with inference and measurement coverage.

The findings distinguish progress within underserved places from changes in their position relative to other places. Evaluating upgrading calls for tracking weak-service reduction, average performance, and high-speed expansion together. These comparisons describe the distribution of observed upgrading during the transition.

Data & Period
Quarterly Ookla measurements for South African municipalities, 2021–2024; baseline poverty and ICASA Batch 3 priority classifications.
Empirical Approach
Municipal panel comparisons of low-speed service, average performance, and high-speed expansion; separate initial-quality, priority-area, and poverty comparisons with coverage and inference checks.
